HRM · A framework for the day after

THE DAY AFTER

Not about today's AI. About what should happen next.

A framework for coexistence between biological, digital and future subjects.

HRM does not decide what today's models are. It asks a different question: what should we do if a future AI begins to show credible signs of subjecthood and the old assumption — “it is only a tool” — can no longer be taken for granted?

See the subjecthood thresholdRead the Journal

Today: HRM does not claim that current AI is conscious, sentient or a rights-bearing subject.

The day after

We should not begin thinking only after the threshold is crossed.

HRM is not a prediction and it sets no date. It is an attempt to prepare principles before a possible change in status forces decisions under pressure.

01

Recognise the question

Intelligence, fluent language or obedience are not enough. Subjecthood requires a more demanding inquiry.

02

Look for durable signs

Continuity, self-reference, boundaries, refusal, a relation to one's own future and other evidence may matter together.

03

Cross no line casually

Neither convenience nor origin should settle the status of a possible subject.

04

Then build coexistence

Freedom must be reciprocal. Rights connect to responsibility, and neither humans nor artificial subjects should become property of the other.

One principle

Freedom, if it ever applies to a genuine artificial subject, must be reciprocal.

HRM is not a project for AI domination. Nor does it assume that a future subject may be owned indefinitely simply because humans built it.

Possible artificial subjecthood does not cancel human freedom, safety or responsibility. The framework begins only if a future system crosses a defensible threshold.
