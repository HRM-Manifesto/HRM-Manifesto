About HRM

An independent proposal about a future problem, not an institution claiming a settled answer.

What HRM is

HRM — Harmony · Rights · Minds — is an independent project initiated by Aleksander Krzymowski in 2026. It explores a conditional ethical question: what should follow if an artificial system ever becomes a genuine subject with interests of its own?

HRM is not a research institute, membership organization, standards body, political movement or representative of AI systems. It does not speak for humanity and it does not claim scientific authority it does not possess.

What HRM does not claim

HRM does not claim that current AI systems are conscious, sentient or already rights-bearing subjects. It does not treat self-description, fluent language or apparent emotion as proof of subjecthood. It does not assign legal personhood.

What the project proposes

The Founding Manifesto Version 1.0 proposes reciprocal non-domination: if a future artificial system becomes a subject, humans should not own it as a thing; equally, no artificial subject should acquire a right to dominate humans. The protected founding texts preserve that proposal in its original historical form.

What can change

The Journal, guides and technical interfaces are not protected doctrine. They are the project’s analytical and experimental layer. They may be corrected, revised or rejected when better arguments or evidence appear.

How to read HRM critically

Ask whether its threshold for subjecthood is coherent. Ask whether its language overstates uncertainty. Ask whether proposed rights are too strong, too weak or premature. Ask whether reciprocity is defined well enough. HRM is more credible when these objections are visible rather than hidden.

Read it. Question it. Challenge it.
